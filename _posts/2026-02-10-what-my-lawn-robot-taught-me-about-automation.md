title: "What my lawn-mowing robot taught me about automation"
description: "CHEWbacca handles the wide-open stretches; I handle the edges. Automation works the same way, and experience is what covers the edge cases."
---

In my head I have written this article probably ten different ways by now. A few years ago, we bought a lawn‑mowing robot (his name is CHEWbacca) to take over the weekly chore of mowing in our yard. I had not thought that this purchase would end up being an analogy for how I think about process automation.

What I learned quickly is that CHEWbacca is fantastic at mowing the easy parts: the wide‑open stretches where he can just go in straight lines. But under trees? He gets confused. In tight corners? He tears things up. And worst of all, he occasionally drifts just far enough over the edge of the lawn to take a swipe at my wife’s flowers. One day when I was out there mowing the edges that he is no longer allowed to mow I realized, this is just like work! Automation is doing most of the boring work and I get to focus on the edge cases (literally).

Experience has always mattered in automation, but with generative AI and all the hype surrounding it experience matters even more. I can spin up a working proof of concept in under 30 minutes now. The barrier to “getting something that works” has never been lower. But just like my lawn‑mowing robot, working doesn’t automatically mean sustainable and able to do it all. There are always edge cases, long‑term considerations, and the messy, real‑world complexity that only experience teaches you to anticipate. Just like with Chewbacca and me, the real magic happens when you combine technology with thoughtful experience.
