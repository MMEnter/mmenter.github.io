This past few weeks I got to spend more time than usual thinking and experiencing the future of coding with GitHub Copilot Pro. I started experiencing “vibe coding” to its fullest extent letting the Agent take over the IDE. When discussing this with colleagues, I caught myself sometimes objected to the term, while other times I proudly said, "I vibe coded that!" After reflecting, I realized I actually have two distinct approaches to developing with my Co-Pilot, in one I let the AI Lead and embrace the "vibe" and in the other I lead and object the vibe :D.

The first is what I call Exploratory Vibe Coding. Wearing my Product Manager hat, I focus on figuring out new features and their complexity not aiming for production-ready code, but exploring possibilities and quickly prototyping ideas. It might take me 30 minutes to build a rough application just to see what's feasible. I'm not concerned about efficiency or style; I'm interested in the user experience, understanding the scope of work, and identifying potential roadblocks early. I keep my experience in the back of my head and let Copilot take the yoke. (I had to include one pun with that name.)

The second is Leveraged Coding, where I use GenAI for targeted asks on a particular problem in an existing codebase—that's when I wear my Developer Hat. GitHub Copilot lets me make these changes significantly faster, and it beats me at implementing with the existing codebase. I'm a lot more critical here, keeping changes incremental to ensure I fully understand what changed. I point it in the right direction by referencing existing libraries or asking it to update a function rather than add a new one. In this approach, I keep full control.

In both scenarios, GitHub Copilot has become a true collaboration partner. I like that I can switch between models from Claude, OpenAI, and Google, using each for its strengths. I use the Planning Agent to help me plan and often find myself impressed by its future considerations. Once I feel comfortable with its approach, I let it implement the solution. The magic isn't the AI - it's knowing when to let it lead and when to steer.
