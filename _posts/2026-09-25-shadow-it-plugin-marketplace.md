description: "Quick scripts quietly become critical workflows. Here's why I'm sharing Claude plugins and skills through a plugin marketplace instead of a shared drive."
---

I don't know how many times I have written a quick script or automation to solve someone's problem and then learned that quick script is now a critical part of that workflow. I have learned that if we don't have an easy centralized way to share these helpers, they start having a life of their own on some shared drive.

I caught myself doing "it" again. I wrote a plugin for Claude, shared it with a coworker so they could use it too, and realized later that day: I'd just done the shadow IT thing again. No documentation. No owner besides me. One message away from becoming someone else's "shared drive script."

I wanted to find a solution that allows us to quickly share plugins and skills with our teams, but also allows us to maintain them and keep them up to date. I wanted a way to make sure that the next person who needs to use it can find it without having to ask me and without having to figure out which version is the right one. That's when I found the Plugin Marketplace for Claude.

Installing is a click instead of a copy-paste. Updating happens in one place instead of forty scattered copies. People can browse all available plugins in one place and an organization can choose to make a marketplace optional for exploration or required for anything that touches real workflows.

I am excited to see how this will evolve. I think it will be a great way to share and maintain skills and plugins. There are many strategic decisions to be made about how to manage and govern these shared resources, but I think this is a step in the right direction.

I would love to hear how others are thinking about sharing and maintaining AI skills and plugins. Are you using the Claude Marketplace, a shared drive, a repository, or something else? How do you ensure that the tools you create are discoverable, maintainable, and useful for your team?
