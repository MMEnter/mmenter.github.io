# Part 3 of 6: The Enterprise Marketplace

*Series: Skills, Plugins, and Governance in the Enterprise*

## Brief

Once plugins exist, organizations need a way to distribute them, and that's what a private marketplace does. The real argument is that a marketplace is a control point, not just a catalog: it decides what's allowed in, which version people get, and how updates arrive. Cover curation, version pinning, and how managed settings tie users to approved sources. Close with the line that control only matters if you know what you're letting in.

## Thesis

A marketplace looks like a distribution tool. Its more important job is deciding what doesn't get distributed.

## Outline

1. **Hook**
   - Plugins spread the way spreadsheets and macros once did: someone shares a link, a colleague installs it, and soon nobody knows what's running where.

2. **What a marketplace is**
   - A catalog Claude fetches plugins from.
   - Public marketplaces vs. Anthropic's official and community marketplaces vs. your own private one.
   - The name tells you who publishes the catalog, not whether each plugin in it is safe.

3. **Distribution benefits**
   - One place to find approved plugins.
   - Consistent install experience.
   - Clear ownership of each plugin.

4. **The marketplace as a control point**
   - **Curation:** what gets listed and who decides.
   - **Version pinning:** people get the version that was approved, not whatever is newest.
   - **Update policy:** when and how changes reach users.
   - **Managed settings:** restricting users to approved marketplace sources.

5. **Structuring an org marketplace (personal example)**
   - How you organized it (by team, function, risk level).
   - Who owns intake and approval.
   - Considerations specific to deploying on third-party inference platforms.

6. **Common mistakes**
   - Treating the marketplace as a dumping ground.
   - Leaving auto-update on for everything.
   - No named owner for each plugin.

7. **Bridge to Part 4**
   - Control only matters if you know what you're letting in. Next: what a plugin can actually do on your machine.
